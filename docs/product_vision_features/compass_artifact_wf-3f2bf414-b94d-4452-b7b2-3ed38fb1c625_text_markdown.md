# Reimagining Translation & Localisation for Regulated Industries: An Agentic-AI-Native Platform and Build-Operate-Transfer Service for Pharma/Life Sciences

## TL;DR
- A clean-sheet, agentic-AI-native language platform — multi-agent orchestration plus accountable human experts — can compress pharma translation cycle times and cost substantially while *strengthening* compliance, because the binding constraints in regulated language work are reviewer bottlenecks, audit traceability and terminology governance, not raw translation speed; incumbent CAT/TMS tools (memoQ, RWS Trados, Phrase, Smartling, Lokalise, XTM) have bolted LLMs onto a segment-and-translation-memory core rather than redesigning the workflow.
- The opportunity is large and defensible: the language services market is roughly USD 71.84bn (2025), life sciences is about 18% of spend and pays a 30–50% per-word premium for regulated work; the regulatory moat (EMA QRD/linguistic review, EU MDR/IVDR, ISPOR linguistic validation, ISO 17100/18587, 21 CFR Part 11, GxP) makes this a poor fit for generic AI translation and an ideal fit for a governed, validated, private-LLM platform.
- ZS should productise this as a Build–Operate–Transfer (BOT) "Language Operations" capability: build the agentic platform and validated workflows, operate it as managed language operations against outcome-based KPIs (cycle time, cost, reviewer hours saved, first-pass quality), then transfer the capability into the client's Global Capability Centre (GCC) — with the same architecture extensible to banking (MiFID II/KID disclosures, KYC/AML) and legal (certified translation, e-discovery, contracts).

## Key Findings

**1. The market is large, growing and structurally premium for regulated content.** The global language services market is projected at USD 71.84bn in 2025, reaching USD 96.97bn by 2031 at a 5.12% CAGR (Mordor Intelligence, Jan 2026 update) — consistent with CSA Research's ~USD 71.7bn (2024/2025) and Nimdzi's tracking; Slator's narrower "language transformation" addressable market is USD 31.70bn. Life sciences captured 18.36% of 2025 spending, "underpinned by multilingual labeling, pharmacovigilance reports, and clinical-trial materials required by the FDA and EMA" (Mordor), and post-edited machine translation already represents about 38.87% of technology investment. Life-science clients accept rates 30–50% higher per word to secure linguists versed in pharmacovigilance and device nomenclature. Regulated medical/clinical translation runs roughly USD 0.20–0.40 per word versus USD 0.12–0.20 for general business content.

**2. The incumbent industry model is being disrupted but not reimagined.** The Nimdzi 2025 Language Technology Radar found TMS vendors rebranding as "AI-powered localisation platforms," but at the core "these platforms still revolve around the same fundamental units: translation segments, translation memories, and workflows. The TMS has not disappeared; it has simply absorbed new technologies and adopted a more marketing-friendly vocabulary." Per Nimdzi senior researcher Laszlo Varga, the industry has "not yet seen... a disruptive innovation that would challenge the create-translate-publishing paradigm." LLMs have been added as a feature layer, not an architecture. MTPE (machine translation post-editing) adoption surged from 26% (2022) to ~46% (2024) per Nimdzi; Slator's 2025 survey found 60% of respondents using MT, rising to 80% among LSPs, with 90–98% of MT/LLM users performing some post-editing. This has compressed translator economics — post-editing typically pays 50–70% of standard rates (some agencies as low as 25%), and a 2025 GTS survey found ~86% of freelancers believe MTPE pricing has worsened. Frey & Llanos-Paredes ("Lost in Translation," Oxford Martin School Working Paper, 2025) found across 695 US local labour markets that "a one percentage point increase in the use of Google Translate reduced translator employment growth by 0.71 percentage points," with "approximately 28,000 fewer translator positions... created" (2010–2023). The signal: buyers increasingly want governed AI + expert review, and the value is migrating from "translation" to orchestration, quality estimation and compliance assurance.

**3. LLMs now beat traditional NMT on the dimensions that matter for regulated content — context, terminology adherence and document-level coherence — but they hallucinate.** WMT25's General MT task pivoted to document-level evaluation; LLM-based systems are strongest for "document-level coherence, tone control, and better handling of context." Lokalise's blind study found LLM output rated "good" 55.7–80% of the time even without context. But LLMs "hallucinate" — inventing terms or facts — "devastatingly risky in legal, medical, or technical domains." This is precisely why an agentic architecture with self-critique, retrieval grounding and mandatory human checkpoints, rather than raw LLM translation, is the right design.

**4. Quality measurement is shifting to MQM + LLM-as-judge, enabling continuous, automated QA.** The Multidimensional Quality Metrics (MQM) framework decomposes quality into a seven-dimension error typology (Accuracy, Fluency, Terminology, Style, Locale, Non-translation, etc.), with major/minor severity weighting producing a numeric score. LLM-as-judge approaches (GEMBA-MQM, Auto-MQM, M-MAD multi-agent debate) now achieve high agreement with human annotators, though research cautions they "fail to reliably capture important language-related and semantic errors" — so human judgement remains indispensable. An agentic platform can run continuous MQM-style quality estimation on every segment and document, routing only genuinely risky content to humans.

**5. The regulatory moat is the core of the thesis.** Pharma language work is governed by an interlocking stack:
- **EMA QRD templates** (current version 10.4) mandate standardised SmPC/PIL/labelling structure across 24 languages with pre-approved "standard phrases" that cannot be paraphrased; using a linguist's own rendering is "a compliance failure."
- **EMA linguistic review process:** after a positive CHMP opinion at Day 210, marketing authorisation holders have just **5 calendar days (to Day 215)** to submit Product Information translated into all 24 EU/EEA languages — a brutal, automatable deadline (member states then review by ~Day 229; final translations at ~Day 235; no further revision after Day +27).
- **EMA readability/user testing:** PILs must be tested with real patients (typically ≥20 participants), with information findable and understandable.
- **EU MDR (Art. 10(11))/IVDR (Art. 10(10)):** device information must be supplied in the official language(s) of each member state where marketed; MDR Art. 41 changed "may require" to "shall be."
- **ISPOR linguistic validation** for PRO/COA instruments: a 10-step process (preparation, dual forward translation, reconciliation, back translation, back-translation review, harmonisation, cognitive debriefing, finalisation, proofreading, report).
- **ISO 17100** (translation services, mandatory second-linguist revision), **ISO 18587** (full post-editing of MT), **ISO 5060** (translation quality evaluation), **ISO 9001/13485/27001**.
- **GxP, 21 CFR Part 11, EU GMP Annex 11** (validated systems, audit trails, electronic signatures, ALCOA+ data integrity), **GAMP 5/CSV/CSA** validation.

**6. Regulators are actively shaping AI use — and the current GxP draft would bar raw LLMs from critical applications.** The **EMA Reflection paper on the use of AI in the medicinal product lifecycle** (EMA/CHMP/CVMP/83833/2023, final adopted 9 September 2024) states that AI used for "drafting, compiling, editing, translating, tailoring, or reviewing medicinal product information documents should be used under close human supervision," because "generative language models are prone to include plausible but erroneous or incomplete output," requiring quality review so model-generated text is "factually and syntactically correct before submission." It mandates a "human-centric approach" and risk-based governance (distinguishing "high patient risk" and "high regulatory impact"). **EU GMP Annex 22 (Artificial Intelligence)** — a draft in joint EU/PIC/S consultation (7 July–7 October 2025, not yet in force) — currently states that "the document does not apply to Generative AI and Large Language Models (LLM), and such models should not be used in critical GMP applications," permitting them only in non-critical applications "with a human-in-the-loop (HITL)"; EMA is reassessing this via a 30 June–1 July 2026 workshop. The **FDA draft guidance** "Considerations for the Use of Artificial Intelligence to Support Regulatory Decision-Making for Drug and Biological Products" (Docket FDA-2024-D-4689, January 2025; comments closed April 2025) proposes a risk-based credibility-assessment framework anchored on an AI model's "context of use." Implication: a compliant platform must be private/validated, deterministic where it matters, fully audit-trailed, and human-accountable — design constraints the incumbents do not natively meet and generic AI translation cannot satisfy.

## Details

### A. Industry pains and structural problems

**Pharma buyer pains.** Multi-country trials must use one primary regulatory language while translating participant-facing materials into every member-state language and back-translating to check fidelity (EU CTR 536/2014). Recurrent pains: (i) **cost** — regulated per-word premiums plus DTP, SME review and certification; (ii) **turnaround** — protocol amendments and country additions trigger urgent retranslation; the EMA Day 215 five-day translation deadline is acute; (iii) **reviewer bottlenecks** — in-country reviewers (ICR), KOLs and affiliate medical/regulatory staff are scarce and tap in late; (iv) **back-translation/reconciliation overhead**, especially for ICFs and PRO/COA; (v) **terminology consistency** across a submission set and across years of versions; (vi) **fragmented vendors/LSPs** with inconsistent quality and weak audit trails; (vii) **version control** across countries and amendments. In commercial content, MLR is the defining bottleneck: Veeva data shows biopharmas take ~3 weeks on average to deliver new content (≈6 days in draft, ~15 days in review), with pre-review adding 5–150 days per asset; optimised MLR workflows have cut review cycle times 57% and review-meeting time 55%.

**Structural industry problems.** The LSP model is people-heavy and margin-compressed (ELIS 2025: ~38% gross margin for translation). Quality measurement is subjective and inconsistently applied. CAT/TMS tools remain segment-and-TM bound; "true end-to-end automation" is rare and integration is painful. TM/termbase maintenance is a perpetual overhead. MTPE has industrialised the work while degrading translator economics and morale — a fragile foundation, since the same experts are needed for high-stakes review.

**Content types and their specific pain points:**
- **Clinical:** ICFs (readability + back-translation + ethics-committee approval), protocols, CRFs, CSRs, patient-facing recruitment materials, PRO/COA (full linguistic validation).
- **Regulatory/labelling:** SmPC, PIL, labelling artwork (QRD templates, standard phrases, readability testing, the Day 215 deadline), country-specific labelling, MDR/IVDR IFUs and implant cards.
- **Pharmacovigilance:** adverse-event narratives, PSURs/DSURs (EMA GVP Module VII), local literature monitoring (MAHs must screen literature and report ICSRs into EudraVigilance); TransPerfect Life Sciences (GlobalLink) states that "leveraging AI for fast, cost-effective translations allows you to meet multilingual reporting requirements while reducing overall cycle times by 40%," alongside "50% faster redactions through pharmacovigilance automation."
- **Medical affairs:** MSL materials, publications, medical-information responses.
- **Commercial:** MLR-reviewed promotional content, transcreation, claims/reference management.
- **Manufacturing/quality:** SOPs, batch records (GMP, Annex 22 scope).

### B. Current technology landscape and limits
CAT tools (Trados, memoQ) give translators TM/termbase/QA infrastructure; cloud TMS (Phrase, Smartling, Lokalise, XTM, Crowdin) add workflow automation and connectors. All now offer LLM pre-translation as a first-pass option (memoQ AGT uses RAG over TM/termbase; Phrase/Smartling/Smartcat have proprietary AI layers; Transifex's TQI and XTM's GenAI score quality; Smartcat Autopilot and Smartling pipelines experiment with content routing). Adaptive-MT specialists (Lilt, Lengoo) pioneered human-in-the-loop "contextual AI" that retrains in real time on linguist edits — Lilt states its Contextual AI Engine "enables translators to work 3-5x faster without sacrificing quality" (Intel's Loïc Dufresne de Virel reported the technology "enabled us to reduce translation costs by 40% year-over-year for the same volume of content"; English-to-German word-prediction accuracy rose from 77.4% to 85.0%) and avoids classic MTPE. **The gap:** these remain segment-centric, retrofit AI onto legacy cores, and were not built for regulated traceability, validated deployment, multi-agent compliance checking, or document/submission-level reasoning. Veeva is the closest analogue in spirit — its December 2025 Vault PromoMats AI agents (Quick Check Agent, Content Agent, forthcoming Agentic MLR) embed compliance-aware agents in core processes — but Veeva targets MLR/content, not the translation/localisation workflow per se.

### C. How agentic AI reimagines the workflow
Instead of a linear segment-by-segment CAT pipeline, an **orchestrator agent** plans the job from content type, risk tier, target locales and regulatory context, then coordinates specialist agents:
- **Ingestion/parsing agent** (Word, XML/structured content, PDF, InDesign/IDML, XLIFF).
- **Terminology agent** (enforces approved termbases incl. EMA QRD standard phrases and MedDRA).
- **Translation agent(s)** (RAG-grounded on TM, termbase, prior approved PI, regulatory corpora).
- **QA/critique agent** (continuous MQM-style scoring, self-critique loops).
- **Compliance/regulatory-check agent** (QRD structure, standard phrases, readability heuristics, MDR/IVDR language matrices, claim/reference linkage).
- **Back-translation & reconciliation agent** (for ICF/PRO workflows).
- **Formatting/DTP agent** (layout fidelity, labelling artwork).
- **ICR-support and query/clarification agent** (drafts reviewer-ready packages, manages queries).

This is grounded in current multi-agent research (TransAgents-style editorial agent teams; translate→critique→refine loops) and orchestration patterns (LangGraph stateful graphs, A2A protocols). Critically for regulated use, the platform must layer **governance** over orchestration: deterministic behaviour where required, full traceability of every agent action, eval frameworks, confidence scoring with low-confidence flagging, and mandatory human checkpoints — directly addressing the EMA reflection paper's "close human supervision" and Annex 22's human-in-the-loop expectations.

### D. Vision: language operations as a governed strategic capability
The future state reframes translation from a procurement line item into a governed capability where agents do the heavy lifting (drafting, terminology enforcement, QA scoring, formatting, back-translation, package assembly, query management) and humans do what only humans should: linguistic validation, domain/medical judgement, in-country regulatory review, exception handling and accountable approval (the named, signing reviewer required by ISO 17100's second-linguist revision and Part 11 e-signatures). The result: faster, cheaper, more consistent and *more auditable* than the status quo.

### E. Product feature architecture and differentiators
Core capabilities: complex-format ingestion; agent-orchestrated translation; reimagined TM/terminology (living, RAG-served, auto-curated); continuous quality estimation with MQM scoring and LLM-as-judge ensembles; compliance/regulatory checks; ICR workflow with reviewer-ready packages; immutable audit trail and traceability; integrations with Veeva Vault (RIM/PromoMats/QualityDocs), CTMS, RIM and eCOA platforms; human-agent collaboration UI; analytics dashboards (cycle time, cost, first-pass quality, reviewer hours); and **private/validated LLM deployment** (EU-hosted data residency, GDPR/HIPAA, IP protection of unpublished clinical/regulatory content, GAMP 5/CSV validation, 21 CFR Part 11/Annex 11 controls). **Differentiators vs memoQ/Trados/Phrase:** agent-native (not segment-native); document/submission-level reasoning; built-in regulatory rule-checking; validated/auditable by design; outcome-based rather than per-word; reimagined rather than retrofitted.

### F. High-value workflows to build
1. **ICF translation + linguistic validation** — agents handle forward translation, back-translation, reconciliation drafting and ethics-committee package assembly; humans validate and approve.
2. **SmPC/PIL/labelling localisation across 24 EU languages** — QRD-structure and standard-phrase enforcement, automated assembly to hit the Day 215 deadline.
3. **PRO/COA linguistic validation** — orchestrating the ISPOR 10-step process with agent support at each step and human cognitive debriefing.
4. **Pharmacovigilance narrative translation & literature monitoring** — fast, audit-trailed AE/PSUR translation and local-literature screening.
5. **MLR-reviewed promotional transcreation** — agentic pre-checks (à la Veeva Quick Check) plus transcreation and claims/reference linkage.
6. **Regulatory submission package localisation** — submission-level terminology consistency and traceability.
7. **Medical information response translation** — rapid, governed responses.

### H. Business model and BOT
ZS's existing **Build–Operate–Transfer–Evolve** GCC model and ZAIDYN platform (with its agentic "Operations Observatory," self-healing agents, Veeva/Salesforce integration) are a natural home. ZS already reports up to 30% operational cost reduction in GCC programmes and works across the industry.
- **Build:** stand up the validated agentic platform, private LLM deployment, termbases, validated workflows and integrations.
- **Operate:** managed language operations against outcome-based SLAs/KPIs — cycle-time reduction, cost-per-outcome, first-pass quality (MQM), reviewer hours saved, compliance-risk reduction, on-time Day 215 delivery.
- **Transfer:** rebadge/transfer people, process and platform into the client's GCC; ZS "Evolve" keeps it current.
- **Commercial model:** shift from per-word to per-outcome/subscription + managed-services fees; value metrics tied to KPIs.
- **Cross-industry extension:** same platform serves **banking** (MiFID II/PRIIPs KID disclosures, KYC/AML, prospectuses — terminology governance, EU-hosted data sovereignty, audit trails) and **legal** (certified/sworn translation, e-discovery, contracts, patents — formatting fidelity, tracked-changes, certification).

## Recommendations
1. **Stage 1 (0–6 months) — Prove a wedge workflow.** Build the agentic pipeline for one high-pain, high-frequency, rules-heavy workflow: **SmPC/PIL labelling localisation (Day 215)** or **ICF + back-translation**. Target a private/validated LLM deployment from day one. Benchmark against incumbent baselines on cycle time, cost, and MQM first-pass quality. *Threshold to proceed:* demonstrable ≥40% cycle-time reduction and ≥30% cost reduction at non-inferior MQM quality with full audit trail.
2. **Stage 2 (6–18 months) — Operate as managed language operations.** Add PV narratives, PRO/COA validation orchestration and MLR transcreation; integrate Veeva Vault and eCOA/CTMS. Sell outcome-based contracts. *Threshold:* sustained KPI delivery, ISO 17100/18587 and CSV/Part 11 validation evidence, and reviewer-hours-saved data sufficient to underwrite BOT economics.
3. **Stage 3 (18–36 months) — Transfer and extend.** Execute BOT handover into the client GCC; replicate into banking and legal verticals. *Threshold:* clean validation/audit record and client capability maturity.
4. **Governance guardrails throughout:** keep humans accountable and signing; maintain determinism/traceability; track the Annex 22 final text and FDA AI guidance finalisation, since a finalised Annex 22 LLM prohibition for *critical* GMP applications would constrain manufacturing-document use and must shape your "critical vs non-critical" content tiering.

## Caveats
- Several market figures are vendor/analyst estimates (Mordor, CSA, Nimdzi, Slator) that differ in scope and methodology; treat ranges, not point estimates, as reliable. The USD 71.84bn figure and 18.36% life-sciences share come from Mordor Intelligence (some Mordor pages cite slightly different figures, e.g. USD 71.82bn growing to USD 93.37bn by 2030 at 5.39% CAGR); Slator's narrower "language transformation" addressable market is USD 31.70bn.
- Some cited efficiency gains (e.g. 40% PV cycle reduction, 57% MLR reduction, Lilt 3–5× productivity / Intel's 40% cost reduction, McKinsey 30–45% linguist productivity) are vendor or single-source claims and should be validated in a controlled pilot before being used in client business cases.
- **Regulatory status is in flux:** EU GMP Annex 22 is a draft (consultation closed Oct 2025; not in force) whose current LLM/GenAI prohibition for critical GMP applications is under active reassessment (EMA workshop 30 June–1 July 2026); the FDA AI guidance is a draft (comments closed April 2025; not finalised as of June 2026). Design for the strict interpretation and adjust as final texts land.
- LLM-as-judge quality metrics, while strong, are documented to miss some semantic/linguistic errors; human validation must remain in the loop for high-risk content.
